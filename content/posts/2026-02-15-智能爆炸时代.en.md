AI's programming capabilities are improving at a crazy pace, reaching a point where all human programmers face obsolescence. After deeply using GLM5 these past few days, I clearly feel: if I were still hand-coding now, it would be no different from being a fool.

Why am I so anxious? The core reason is that I'm a formally trained graduate—I studied software engineering for 7 years during my undergraduate and graduate studies. I know deeply that this discipline used to be a practice-intensive and time-consuming field: you need to spend thousands or even tens of thousands of hours repeatedly coding, trial-and-error, and debugging to grow from a novice engineer to a senior engineer. And in this process, a large amount of time is wasted on meaningless debugging and manual coding. This is also why I don't like programming—I'm fundamentally not someone who enjoys repetitive work.

But after 2023, this discipline has been completely transformed. Large language models have been continuously improving on various programming metrics, especially since the second half of 2025, AI's programming capabilities have far exceeded human levels and can tangibly achieve ROI improvements. Recalling the earlier judgment of Anthropic's CEO, there's no problem with it: by the end of 2026, 80%-90% of the world's new code may be generated by AI.
