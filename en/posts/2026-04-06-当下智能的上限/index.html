The Upper Limit of Current Intelligence

April 6, 2026 · 2 min · Honghui Liu | Translations:
  • 中文

The anxiety about AI stems from the unclear boundaries of artificial intelligence capabilities, especially as AI’s capabilities have been exponentially increasing in recent years. The most notable is in mathematics and programming, areas that humans have traditionally used as intelligence benchmarks, which are the easiest for AI to overcome. This involves the fundamental understanding of human intelligence versus AI intelligence. From this dimension, human intelligence is more like general intelligence, while AI intelligence is just specialized intelligence.

A few days ago I saw a great perspective: current AI is more like a mediocre consensus, while humanity’s most precious ability is the flash of insight that can break these consensuses. Combined with Hassabis’s earlier definition of AGI, going back to 1911, AI should be able to autonomously derive Einstein’s theory of relativity. If AI cannot break this capability boundary, then its upper limit is deterministic. But even so, such an upper limit would still be a tremendous improvement in productivity efficiency.

So for us, what we need to think about is spending our time on more meaningful capability building, such as continuous thinking ability, rather than skill proficiency. Many professions will face reshaping, and professions themselves are intermediate products in the process of social development. What professions will still exist in ten or twenty years, and still have value, is a question that requires our serious consideration.
